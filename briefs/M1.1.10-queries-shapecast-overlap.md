## Context

Eleventh core sub-milestone of the M1.1 rigid arc, and the second and last that interrogates the world instead of advancing it. M1.1.9 implemented the raycast and froze the complete family; this milestone replaces the five `@panic` bodies it left behind. The normative model for the four non-ray families was authored for this milestone and is `engine-physics-forge.md` §1.11.10 through §1.11.14, appended to the §1.11 series so that nothing renumbers.

Three things are settled here that are larger than the five bodies.

First, **a shape cast is not expressible over the ray kernels.** The cast of A along `d` against B is a raycast against the Minkowski difference of the two cores, inflated by `r_a + r_b` (§1.11.11). A sphere cast against a box is therefore a ray against a *rounded* box — exactly the shape `rayShape` rejects with `error.UnsupportedShape`. The cast gets its own kernel, and because that kernel covers every bounded convex through its support map, it has no shape to reject and needs no error channel. The frozen signature, which carries none, is evidence for the design rather than a constraint on it.

Second, **the solver-side entries of the whole family move to the solver scalar.** M1.1.9 left the five stubs typed on the `f32` public aggregates while the implemented raycast trio takes `Real`, and its own pin test records that one of the two halves would have to move here or at the freeze. It moves here. An entry typed `f32` inside the solver would force the conversion *into* the solver — under `-Dphysics_f64`, a time of impact and a contact point narrowed to `f32` before leaving the kernel and widened again at the interface tier: two conversions, one of them invisible, and the loss of precisely what the flag buys. The public `f32` surface of `engine-tier-interfaces.md` §1 and `api/types.zig` is **not touched**; it is wrapped in one place, at M1.1.15, for all eight entries at once. `engine-physics-forge.md` §1.11.8 carries the corollary.

Third, **`BodyId` cannot order a query result.** It is a slot index: it encodes creation order. The three overlap entries have no distance to rank by, so an unranked truncation had no invariant key at all; and the ray family's `(distance, BodyId)` key is already wrong on an exact distance tie. Measured on `main` at `deeabe7`, `f32`: two unit spheres on either side of the ray at a perpendicular offset of 0.5 give the bit-identical distance `19.133974`, and swapping the two creation orders changes the entity returned — for the closest hit as much as for the answer truncated to one slot. The key becomes `(distance, entity, BodyId)` across the family, `BodyId` surviving only as the final tie-break. `engine-physics-forge.md` §1.11.14 is normative on this.

## Scope

- **`src/foundation/math/aabb.zig`** — `Aabb(T).inflate(half_extents: Vec3T) Self`: the box grown by `half_extents` on every axis. Added here under the first-consumer rule, the `surfaceArea()` (M1.1.1) and `rayInterval()` (M1.1.9) precedents. An addition, not a threshold: no constant appears.
- **`Bvh(T).queryCast(ray, extent, collector) u32`** and **`Broadphase(T).queryCast(ray, extent, collector) u32`** in `pipeline/broadphase.zig` — the swept traversal of §1.11.10. Additive on `queryRay`: the same duck-typed collector contract including `maxDistance()` and `shouldStop()`, the same near-first descent, the same slab test unchanged, the same visited-node return. The one difference is that each node's stored box is inflated by `extent` before the slab test. `queryRay` is re-expressed as `queryCast` with a zero extent and its behaviour must stay **bit-identical**: the existing M1.1.9 traversal suite is the proof and must not be weakened to accommodate the refactor.
- **`src/modules/forge/forge_3d/pipeline/narrowphase/shapecast.zig`** (create) — the GJK cast kernel: a raycast from the origin against the Minkowski difference of the two cores, inflated by the radius sum, in the core + inflation-radius convention and never a second one. Returns the time of impact, the witness point on B, and the separating axis. Reuses `Simplex(T)`'s Voronoi closest-origin solver and `support.zig` (`SupportShape`, `RelativePose`, `minkowskiSupport`); it does **not** call `gjk()`. Dependency discipline identical to `raycast.zig` and `fast_paths.zig`: imports `foundation` and the sibling `support.zig` only — never `gjk.zig`'s loop, `manifold.zig`, `epa.zig`, `weld_forge`, `body*.zig`, `config.zig` or `broadphase.zig`. A named iteration ceiling, sibling of `max_gjk_iterations`, whose exhaustion returns a hit at the current parameter (§1.11.11).
- **`src/modules/forge/forge_3d/query/`** (create as a package; `query.zig` moves into it) — `root.zig` is the façade carrying the eight public entries, the shared `Filter`, `prepare`, the solver-side mirror types and the ordering key; `ray.zig` holds the three ray collectors moved unchanged from `query.zig`; `cast.zig` and `overlap.zig` hold the new collectors and per-candidate evaluation. `forge_3d/root.zig` re-exports the package at the same public path, so `forge_3d.query.raycast` is unchanged for every caller. The move is a `git mv` plus a split, not a rewrite: the ray code that moves must move **textually unchanged** except for the ordering-key edit of E1, so the diff is reviewable.
- **The five bodies, at `Real`** — `shapeCast`, `overlapShape`, `overlapAabb`, `pointQuery`, `closestPoint`, with solver-side mirror types `CastQuery`, `CastHit`, `OverlapRequest`, `ClosestPointHit` alongside the existing `RayQuery` / `RayHit`, and `[]BodyId` outputs. `overlapAabb` gains `store: *const ShapeStore`, without which its exact kernel is unreachable (see Notes). Every entry keeps taking `*const BodyManager`: a query cannot wake anything, structurally.
- **`BodyManager` adapters** — `entity(id) ?EntityId` (the column exists on `Body` and was never exposed); `castShapeBody`, `overlapShapeBody`, `containsPointBody`, `closestPointBody`, each resolving the body's pose and support shape via `store` and running the exact kernel, mirroring `raycastBody` / `gjkPair` / `collidePair`. Each returns null on a stale handle or a stale shape.

## Out of scope

- **Analytic swept fast paths.** One generic kernel. Per-pair analytic sweeps are the M1.1.4 pattern — a separate body of work carrying a geometric-equivalence obligation against the generic path — and doubling the acceptance surface in one session is what the one-milestone-one-session rule exists to prevent. Additive after profiling.
- **The deepest point at a zero time of impact.** It requires EPA; it is purely additive (a defaulted field, zero call sites), on the model of the `treat_convex_as_solid` toggle, and the reference itself gates it behind a setting.
- **A bounded nearest-neighbour descent for `closestPoint`.** `max_distance` is a required parameter, so the caller already bounds the candidate set. Additive.
- **Widening the public surface.** The `f32` boundary is engine-wide; widening is one decision over `BodyDescriptor`, the interface pose, the query results and the ECS `Transform` together, and belongs to M1.1.15. Moving the *solver-side* entries to `Real` is not that decision and must not be presented as it: `engine-tier-interfaces.md` §1 and the public types of `api/types.zig` are untouched.
- **Plane, MeshShape and every shape beyond sphere/box/capsule** (M1.1.11), and therefore back-face handling, vacuous on a solid convex. Note what the kernel does and does not buy: it extends to any *bounded* convex through its support map, so ConvexHull (M1.1.19) is nearly free, but an infinite half-space has an **unbounded** support map and a non-convex MeshShape needs a per-triangle traversal. Neither is free and neither is here.

- Far-field conditioning beyond what §1.11.4 bis mandates. It is characterised and **not** fixed; a compensated intermediate is roughly twice the cost of the hot-path dot products and belongs to M1.1.15.
- `step()` / `PhysicsWorld` / `PhysicsModule` instantiation / ECS `Transform` sync (M1.1.15), the Tier 1 `physics_query` service wiring and its Etch wrappers, and the entity-level deduplication that service owes (§13).

## Notes

**Threshold discipline.** A cast is pure geometry with one extra temporal parameter, which is twice the ground on which this discipline is usually lost. Every guard is at **true zero**. In particular the "no longer approaching" test: the reference compares the axis-direction product against `-1.0e-18f` so that the following division cannot overflow, but at true zero that division is never reached, and a denormal negative denominator overflows the step to infinity, hence the parameter past `max_distance`, hence a miss through the existing bound test. No NaN is reachable on that path, the numerator being strictly positive at that branch. Write that argument next to the guard. Same substitution as §1.11.2 against the absolute parallel guard of `RayAABox.h`. No geometric epsilon anywhere; distances compared exactly, including in the tie-break.

**The iteration ceiling is normative, not an implementation choice.** §1.11.11 carries the table. Returning a miss on exhaustion fabricates a false negative, therefore tunneling; returning the current parameter fabricates a contact announced **early**, never a missed one, which is the safe failure direction for the character controller of M1.1.12. The parameter grows from zero and is at every step a lower bound of the true time of impact, which is what makes the early answer conservative rather than merely wrong.

**Reference lineage, verified on source at `jrouwe/JoltPhysics@master`, `Jolt/Geometry/GJKClosestPoint.h`.** `CastShape` builds the Minkowski difference of B and the transformed A and calls its own `CastRay`, credited to *Ray Casting against General Convex Objects with Application to Continuous Collision Detection*, Gino van den Bergen. That `CastRay` has **no iteration ceiling at all** (`for (;;)`), and both of its non-convergence exits — the parameter failing to progress, and the simplex reduction failing after the single permitted restart — break out as a **hit at the current parameter**, the second with the comment that a state too close to resolve is returned as a hit. The radius-carrying `CastShape` overload keeps the support points on A and B separate, reconstructs `outPointB` from the simplex barycentrics **in every case including the full simplex**, and computes A's witness separately only when the parameter is zero — beyond that the two coincide and only B's is computed, as it carries fewer terms. The separating axis falls back to the **previous iteration's** axis when the radius sum is zero and the current axis collapses at convergence. In `Jolt/Physics/Collision/CollideShape.h`, `mPenetrationAxis` is documented as the direction to move shape 2 out of collision and the recommended contact normal is its negated normalisation — that is, the outward normal of the hit body, which is the Weld convention. Verify any further claim about the reference on the reference, never on a comment in this repository.

**Why `position = cast.origin` is refused at a zero time of impact.** The analogy with the ray does not hold. A ray whose origin is inside a shape has its origin *in the hit body* by construction; two overlapping volumes can have the cast shape's centre entirely outside the hit body. Returning the origin would yield a point that is neither a contact point nor a point of the collider, contradicting §13's public surface and the normal convention. The witness on B comes out of the same loop; no EPA is involved.

**Why `overlapAabb` needs `store`, and why the fat boxes are not the answer.** The trees store boxes fattened by `BroadphaseConfig.margin`, default `0.1 m`. Returning the candidate set would report bodies that do not overlap the query box, and — the decisive point — the error would be a function of a **tuning constant**: changing the margin would change a query's answer. The exact kernel is therefore the body's tight world AABB, which `BodyManager.bodyAabb` computes from the shape and the pose and which consequently needs `store`. The M1.1.9 signature was declared without it on the belief, recorded in that milestone's tag annotation, that the entry stops at the broadphase. It stops at AABB *granularity*, which is not the same thing (§1.11.12).

**`.shallow` is not an interior.** `GjkResult.closest_a` / `closest_b` are valid for `.separated` **and** `.shallow`, and `.shallow` means the cores are disjoint beyond the contact margin while the inflated surfaces touch or overlap — a real separation absorbed by the numeric margin. Collapsing it with `.deep` would return a zero distance for a measurably external point. Solidity is decided by `containsPoint`, upstream of any classification, and the surface projection then runs uniformly over both valid regimes (§1.11.13).

**`BodyId` encodes creation order, and this was measured, not argued.** On `main` at `deeabe7`, at `f32`, two unit spheres at `(20, ±0.5, 0)` against a ray from the origin along `+X` both return `19.133974`, bit-identical because the squared perpendicular offset is `0.25` on both sides; the closed form is `20 − √0.75 = 19.133974596`, whose nearest `f32` is `19.133974075`. Swapping the two creation orders changes the entity returned by `raycast` and by `raycastAll` truncated to one slot. A symmetric scene is not a measure-zero case: mirrored cover or props on a grid produce it. The named residual stands: two bodies carrying the **same** entity at an exactly equal distance fall back on `BodyId` and are not invariant.

**The traversal starts at the AABB centre, not at the shape's position.** They coincide for the three shapes in the store, whose local AABB is centred on the origin, but that is a property of those shapes and not of the model; a decentred volume would break the equality. Write it on the centre, with the line saying why the two agree today. The returned parameter is the same either way, the sweep being a pure translation.

**Do not reach for a single swept AABB.** Enclosing the whole sweep in one box and calling the overlap traversal loses the bound entirely and turns a sub-linear query into one linear in the proxies of the swept corridor.

**Do not write analytic swept fast paths.** Out of scope, and the reason is the M1.1.4 precedent: a fast path owes a geometric-equivalence proof against the generic path, which is a body of work of its own.

**`subshape_id` and `cast_subshape_id` stay 0.** One shape is one body until compounds (M1.1.20). The material is derived at service level from the sub-shape identity and never carried in the solver result (§1.11.7).

**File length.** The `query/` package exists so that no file in it approaches the 500-line review guideline of `engine-zig-conventions.md`. `broadphase.zig` and `epa.zig` are already conscious brief-driven overages; a third avoidable one is not justified.

**Step 3 — the key corrected.** `hitLess` becomes `(distance, entity, BodyId)` over a
`entityKey` helper, and `ClosestCollector.add` now *calls* `hitLess` instead of
re-deriving the comparison inline — so `closest` and an `all` truncated to one slot
cannot disagree structurally, rather than by two hand-written comparisons agreeing.
Two design points, both inside the FROZEN scope's mirror-type mandate:

- **The solver-side `RayHit` gains `entity: EntityId`.** The alternative was passing
  the `BodyManager` into the comparator, which would turn each of
  `AllCollector`'s replace-worst comparisons into a store lookup. The public
  `RaycastHit` carries the entity too, so the mirror is not widened by holding it,
  and the key stays a pure function of two hits.
- **`entityKey` is `index` major, `generation` minor, and deliberately not
  `@bitCast`.** `EntityId` is a `packed struct(u64)` whose `index` occupies the LOW
  32 bits, so a bitcast would order by generation first — a layout accident. Both
  orders satisfy what §1.11.14 requires (a function of the entity identity alone);
  this one also reads the way entities are named. `EntityId.dead` sorts last with no
  special case.
