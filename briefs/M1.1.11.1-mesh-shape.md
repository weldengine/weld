## Context

Thirteenth core sub-milestone of the M1.1 rigid arc, and the **twelfth and last shape** of the C1.1 list. The M1.1.11 plan row originally grouped the infinite plane with the static mesh; it was split, and this is the second half. The plane half shipped a taxonomy enum and an unbounded-shape path; none of that carries over except the enum and one reused error.

The normative model was authored for this milestone and is `engine-physics-forge.md` **§1.11.17**, appended to the §1.11 series so that nothing renumbers. Read it before anything else. `engine-tier-interfaces.md` is at **version 0.5** and carries the descriptor payload and the back-face field.

**This milestone is not split, and the size rule was checked rather than invoked.** `engine-development-workflow.md` §2.2 targets 500–2000 delivered lines including tests. Measured on the four preceding sub-milestones: M1.1.8 delivered 3237 Zig lines, M1.1.9 3968, M1.1.10 5095, M1.1.11 4607. All four exceed the target by 1.6× to 2.5×, all four were reviewed gate by gate and merged. This milestone is estimated at 3400–3900, i.e. **below M1.1.10 and comparable to M1.1.9 and M1.1.11**. It is a normal-sized sub-milestone for this arc, not an outlier, and it ships whole. There is no M1.1.11.2 and none is to be proposed.

Four things are settled here that are larger than one shape.

First, **a mesh is a surface, not a solid, and that is categorical.** Membership is false everywhere; `pointQuery` never returns a body carrying a mesh; `closestPoint` measures to the surface and is never zero by interiority. There is no mesh counterpart to `treat_convex_as_solid`. The reference has one — `Shape::sCollidePointUsingRayCast`, hit-count parity along a `+Y` ray — and Weld refuses it: parity presumes a **closed** mesh, nothing validates closure, on an open mesh the answer then depends on an arbitrary ray direction, and a membership test would cost a full raycast.

Second, **the triangle enters as a fourth `Core` variant, not as a family of kernels.** A triangle is a *bounded* convex whose support map is the max of three dot products — the only property GJK, EPA, the manifold generator and the M1.1.10 cast kernel require. Those four then serve the mesh **unchanged**. Only the ray kernel gains an analytic arm, for the reason §1.11.3 already gives ray kernels their existence: a configuration-space ray march against a *flat* core is ill-conditioned where ray↔triangle has a closed form. This is one variant against four reused families, and it is the reason this milestone is 3900 lines and not 8000.

Third, **the compile-error safety net advertised at M1.1.11 has four holes, and this milestone is where they bite.** `shape.zig` states that every `switch` on `ShapeClass` is exhaustive with no `else` arm, so that adding the third variant is a compile error at each site owing a decision. Verified on `main`: eight sites are real exhaustive switches and will break correctly; **four are not** — `addBody` (`if (class == .half_space and body_type != .static)`), `bodyAabb` (`assert(class == .convex)`), `closestPointBody` (`if (class == .half_space)` then falling through to `supportShape`), `worldAabb` (`assert(class == .convex)`). The fall-through in `closestPointBody` is the dangerous one: it panics in Debug and ReleaseSafe and is undefined behaviour in ReleaseFast. These four are fixed in this milestone, and the docstring is corrected to match what the code does. By contrast the six `switch` sites on `Core` **are** all exhaustive — verified — so the triangle variant is greeted correctly everywhere.

Fourth, **the back-face field is the last thing that can still land on the frozen surface.** §1.11.4's row was amended to say so: the bound is this shape, not the end of Phase 1, the mesh being itself Phase 1. After the M1.1.15 freeze the field cannot land at all. It ships here or never.

## Scope

- **`src/modules/forge/api/types.zig`** — the `ShapeDescriptor.triangle_mesh` payload `{ vertices: []const Vec3, indices: []const u32 }`, borrowed for the duration of the call; and `BackFaceMode { ignore, collide }` with `back_face_mode: BackFaceMode = .ignore` on `RaycastQuery`, `ShapeCastQuery` and `OverlapQuery`. Both are pre-freeze extensions, permitted by `engine-tier-interfaces.md` §1. The three entries that do **not** carry the field carry it for a stated reason (§1.11.17); do not add it to them for symmetry.
- **`ShapeClass` gains `.triangle_soup`** — the category name §1.11.3 and §1.11.15 already use, not `.mesh`. HeightField joins the same category at its own sub-milestone. Every switch stays exhaustive with no `else` arm.
- **The four non-exhaustive class sites become dispatches.** `addBody`, `bodyAabb`, `closestPointBody`, `worldAabb`, plus the two class asserts in `body.zig`. A mesh **has** a valid `local_aabb` — unlike a half-space — so these are not "extend the assert", they are "the mesh has an answer and it differs".
- **Owned memory in the shape store.** A mesh owns its vertices, its indices, its acceleration nodes and its edge flags. `createShape` becomes **transactional**: reserve every allocation before mutating any slot, so an OOM never leaves a half-built mesh in a live slot. `destroyShape` frees; `ShapeStore.deinit` walks live slots. The public signature `destroyShape: fn (*Impl, ShapeId) void` is **unchanged** — the interface tier holds the allocator and supplies it.
- **Descriptor domain, refused by typed error and never sanitised.** Rejected at creation: an index count not a multiple of three, an index outside the vertex array, a non-finite vertex, a mesh with no triangle, and a triangle whose cross product `(v₁−v₀) × (v₂−v₀)` is **exactly** zero — the true-zero guard of §1.11.4 on its largest absolute component, applied verbatim. A sliver of tiny but non-zero area normalises exactly and **must be served**; no area threshold appears anywhere. The reference sanitises (`MeshShapeSettings::Sanitize`); Weld refuses, because removal **renumbers** and that number **is** the `subshape_id`.
- **`forge_3d/mesh.zig`** (create) — `MeshData`: owned arrays, the static binned-SAH builder into a flat node array, the adjacency/active-edge build, and the explicit-stack traversals (ray, swept, AABB, bounded-distance). **Traversal is by explicit fixed-depth stack, never recursion**: the `Bvh` recursion is safe only because its rotation balancing bounds its height, and a static build offers no such guarantee. The depth is a build-time invariant the builder knows and asserts, and the stack is sized on it.
- **Adjacency is built at creation, inside the same transaction.** Pairing is by **sorting** `(min_index, max_index)` keys and pairing adjacent entries — **no hashed container**, the `computePairs` pattern verbatim and the same reason (§1.5). Three flags per triangle, bit 0 for edge `v₀v₁`, bit 1 for `v₁v₂`, bit 2 for `v₂v₀`. An unpaired edge — the boundary of an open mesh — is **active**. Building it in a later gate would reopen the OOM transaction a second time; it belongs to storage.
- **`pipeline/narrowphase/triangle.zig`** (create) — the analytic ray↔triangle kernel and the back-face predicate. Dependency discipline identical to `plane.zig`: imports `foundation` and the sibling `support.zig` only — never `gjk.zig`, `epa.zig`, `manifold.zig`, `weld_forge`, `body*.zig`, `config.zig` or `broadphase.zig`. A triangle core with a non-zero inflation radius is an **asserted precondition** of the ray arm, the same form as the rounded box.
- **`SupportShape.Core` gains `triangle`** in `support.zig`, and the six exhaustive switches on it each gain their arm.
- **Back-face semantics, written once and shared by three entries.** Front face is the side the outward normal points to. For a ray and for a sweep, the hit is a back-face hit when `n · direction > 0`. For an overlap, the triangle is back-facing when the probe lies **entirely** in the rear half-space of its plane: `n · support_probe(n) − r_probe < n · v₀`, one support call, §1.11.15's construction applied to the triangle's plane. A probe straddling the plane touches from the front and counts in both modes.
- **A back-face hit returns a flipped normal.** §1.11.4 declares `normal · direction <= 0` on **all** hits, and the `−direction` choice at distance zero draws its justification from it. Returning the unflipped outward normal would puncture it. The returned normal is `−n`, for the ray family and for the shape cast. Assumed divergence from the reference, which returns it unflipped.
- **`subshape_id` is threaded end to end.** The mesh is the first shape carrying sub-shapes; being root, its path **is** the triangle index. Today the field is written nowhere: `LocalHit` carries only `distance` and `normal`, and `evaluate` in `query/ray.zig` never sets it. `LocalHit`, `BodyCastHit` and `BodyClosestPoint` gain it, and the adapters and collectors carry it to `RaycastHit`, `ShapeCastHit` and `ClosestPointResult`.
- **A mesh returns one hit per body, not one per triangle.** The nearest accepted triangle wins, deduplicated at the collector. The reason is not performance: §1.11.14's key `(distance, entity, BodyId)` does not discriminate two triangles of one body, so two hits would be neither ordered nor invariant and their truncation arbitrary.
- **Contact generation, `collidePair` from 2×2 to 3×3.** Mesh × convex produces **several manifolds per body pair**, one per contacting triangle — the only shape change the mesh imposes on the rigid solver. The contact cache key has carried an unused `subshape_id` at 0 since M1.1.6; this milestone fills it, and warm-starting becomes per triangle and survives a re-traversal. The constraint build loop consumes the collection.
- **Active edges are consumed at contact time.** A contact whose normal comes from an inactive edge has its normal corrected to the face normal. The threshold is a **named, configurable physical parameter** with default `cos(5°)`, the class of `restitution_threshold` and `penetration_slop` — it selects a modelling behaviour, not a numerical tolerance, and §1.11.2's `k · floatEps(T) · coordScale` discipline does **not** apply to it. Say so in the code, at the declaration.
- **Mesh × mesh and mesh × half-space are asserted preconditions of `collidePair`.** Not a structural property: no `BodyType → BroadphaseLayer` mapping exists on `main` — the layer is an insertion argument, `gjk_test.zig` inserts static bodies into `.dynamic`, and the wiring arrives with `PhysicsWorld` at M1.1.15. The motive is that the adapter presumes its pair came from `computePairs` under a correct layer assignment, and a static↔static pair is a programming error at the call site. Write **that** motive, not the other one.
- **A mesh is an inadmissible probe.** `probeAdmissible` returns false for `.triangle_soup`, `error.UnsupportedShape` on `shapeCast` and `overlapShape`. §1.11.7's fourth principle already states it.
- **Normals are not precomputed.** Precomputing does not improve the conditioning — the same cancellation happens at build time — it only costs memory on the heaviest shape in the store. Determinism is satisfied by the **fixed evaluation order** `(v₁−v₀) × (v₂−v₀)`.
- **`shape.zig`'s docstring on `ShapeClass` is corrected** to state what the code does after this milestone, not what M1.1.11 hoped it did.

## Acceptance criteria

### Tests

RED-first on every pinnable behaviour. Every expectation is a **closed form computed in the comment above it**, never a value read back from the implementation.

**`tests/mesh_test.zig`** (create)

*Descriptor domain*
- `test "an index count not a multiple of three is refused"`, `test "an out-of-range index is refused"`, `test "a non-finite vertex is refused"`, `test "an empty mesh is refused"` — typed errors, one per condition, each distinguishable from the others.
- `test "an exactly degenerate triangle is refused and a sliver is served"` — the discriminating pair. The sliver's area is tiny but non-zero, its normal normalises exactly, and the test asserts a **unit** normal on it. A suite that only rejects degenerates would pass with an area threshold in the code; this one will not.

*Owned memory*
- `test "createShape is transactional under OOM"` — a failing allocator at each successive allocation point in turn; after every failure the store holds no live mesh slot and leaks nothing.
- `test "destroyShape frees and deinit walks live slots"` — proven with a leak-checking allocator whose `safety` is **forced true**, and proven in both directions by reintroducing a leak deliberately, once, and reporting that it fired.

*Static acceleration structure*
- `test "the built tree is a partition of the triangle set"` — every triangle in exactly one leaf, leaf boxes contained in their parents, root box equal to the tight bound of all vertices.
- `test "traversal agrees with brute force"` — the load-bearing test of the whole structure. On a pseudo-random mesh of a few hundred triangles, every ray / swept / AABB / bounded-distance traversal returns exactly the brute-force answer over all triangles. Run it over enough seeds that a wrong slab test or a wrong descent order cannot survive.
- `test "the stack depth invariant holds on an adversarial mesh"` — a triangle set chosen to degrade the SAH split; the asserted depth bound holds and the traversal does not overflow.

*Adjacency*
- `test "edge pairing is exact on a closed mesh"` — every edge paired exactly once.
- `test "a boundary edge of an open mesh is active"`.
- `test "a concave edge is inactive and a convex edge past the threshold is active"` — closed forms on a two-triangle fold, at the threshold and one step either side.
- `test "pairing is order-independent"` — the same mesh with permuted triangle order gives bit-identical flags.

*The eight entries*
- `test "raycast hits the nearest triangle and reports its index"` — `subshape_id` equal to the triangle index, verified against the closed form, not read back.
- `test "raycastAll returns one entry per body, not one per triangle"` — a ray crossing several triangles of one mesh yields exactly one entry.
- `test "shapeCast against a mesh"` — closed-form time of impact for sphere, box and capsule probes; a receding direction that misses; a grazing direction whose guard is at true zero.
- `test "overlapShape against a mesh"`, `test "overlapAabb stops at AABB granularity"` — the latter asserting the body **is** returned for a box that overlaps the mesh's AABB but no triangle, so the documented approximation is pinned rather than accidentally tightened.
- `test "pointQuery never returns a mesh body"` — including a point inside the volume a closed mesh encloses. This is the categorical rule, and it is the test that fails if someone adds solidity later.
- `test "closestPoint measures to the surface"` — outside and inside a closed mesh both give the distance to the nearest triangle, never zero by interiority.
- `test "a mesh probe is refused, not silently empty"` — `shapeCast` and `overlapShape`.

*Back faces*
- `test "a ray from behind is ignored by default and hit under collide"` — both verdicts, same geometry.
- `test "a back-face hit returns a flipped normal"` — `normal · direction <= 0` asserted on **every** hit of the suite, both modes, ray and cast.
- `test "a shape cast from behind honours the mode"`.
- `test "an overlap probe entirely behind is discarded, straddling is not"` — the two-sided case is the one that fails if the predicate is written as a sign test on the closest point instead of on the support.

*Contacts and active edges*
- `test "a mesh and a convex produce one manifold per contacting triangle"` — count and identity, closed form.
- `test "warm starting is per triangle"` — the cache key carries the triangle index; a re-traversal that reorders the candidates still warm-starts each contact from its own history.
- `test "a slider does not catch on an internal edge"` — the plan row's own criterion. A box or capsule driven across a flat mesh seam over many steps: the velocity loss across the seam is bounded, and the test asserts that bound rather than eyeballing a trajectory. Assert it **fails** with active-edge correction disabled, so the test proves the mechanism and not the geometry.
- `test "mesh versus mesh and mesh versus half-space are unreachable"` — the precondition asserted, exercised in the debug build.

*Shared, in the suite that owns each entry*
- `test "the object mask filters"` and `test "exclusions are honoured"` — including a mesh body, for each entry.
- `test "a sleeping body answers and stays asleep"` — with a mesh in the scene.
- `test "the result is invariant under creation-order permutation"` — including the identity of the bodies returned.
- `test "two identical runs are bit-identical"`.
- `test "an oblique far-field configuration keeps a unit normal"` — the §1.11.4 bis obligation. ## Notes

**The inflation radius is a failure mode again, in a new place.** `SupportShape.support` returns the support of the **core**. A triangle's core is the triangle and its radius is zero, so the triangle side is easy — but the *probe* side is not, and the back-face predicate `n · support_probe(n) − r_probe < n · v₀` carries the probe's radius explicitly. Written without `−r_probe` it is correct for a box and silently wrong for a sphere and a capsule, by exactly the radius. The M1.1.11 brief said the same thing about the plane's separation formula, and it was right then.

**Threshold discipline, and the one exception.** Every numerical guard on this shape is at **true zero**: the degenerate-triangle test, the ray's parallel test, the cast's grazing test. No geometric epsilon appears in `triangle.zig` or `mesh.zig`. The active-edge threshold is **not** a numerical guard — it is a named physical parameter selecting a modelling behaviour, defaulting to `cos(5°)`, and it lives with `restitution_threshold` and `penetration_slop`. Declare it there and say why, because a reviewer applying §1.11.2's `k · floatEps(T) · coordScale` rule to it will call it a violation and be wrong.

**Recursion is the trap.** The `Bvh` traverses recursively and is safe doing so because rotation balancing bounds its height. Copying that shape into the mesh traversal is the single most likely way this milestone ships a stack overflow that no test on a well-formed mesh will find. Explicit stack, fixed depth, asserted at build.

**Adjacency belongs to gate one, not to the contacts gate.** It is tempting to build the edge flags where they are first consumed. Doing so reopens `createShape`'s OOM transaction a second time and reorders the ownership set of `MeshData` after it has been tested. Build it with the storage; consume it later.

**One hit per body is a decision, not an optimisation.** If the implementation finds itself wanting to return two, the ordering key is the thing that would have to change, and it is not changing here.

## Recorded deviations

**RD-1 — the active-edge threshold is declared in `mesh.zig`, not beside
`restitution_threshold` and `penetration_slop` in `rigid/solver_config.zig`** (gate B).
The brief and §1.11.17 place it in that family, and it belongs to it: it is a named
physical parameter selecting a modelling behaviour, and §1.11.2's
`k · floatEps(T) · coordScale` discipline does not apply to it. But the flags are BAKED
AT CREATION, so a field on `SolverConfig` would be read long after the decision it
governs had been taken and changing it at runtime would silently rebuild nothing; and
`mesh.zig` is shape storage at the `forge_3d/` level, so importing the rigid branch's
config from it would invert the dependency. It is therefore declared as
`mesh.default_active_edge_cos_threshold`, taken as a per-mesh parameter by
`MeshData.init` (which is what makes it configurable), and `solver_config.zig` carries a
doc cross-reference naming it as the third member of the family — so the family stays
readable from one place, which is what "declared with them" is for.

**RD-1 amended at Guy's finding (gate B fix).** The "configurable" claim was FALSE as
first delivered: the only caller path to `MeshData.init` is `createShape(gpa, desc)`, and
the descriptor carried no threshold, so the store passed the default and nothing could
override it — a window that closes for good at the M1.1.15 freeze. Corrected:
`ShapeDescriptor.triangle_mesh` gains `active_edge_cos_threshold: f32` defaulted to the
`f32` rendering of `cos(5°)`, `createShape` passes the descriptor's field and never the
constant, and `default_active_edge_cos_threshold` becomes `f32` — widened once at `init` —
so a build configuration is not an input to the geometry.

**RD-2 — the parity half of that finding is REFUTED, measured in the repo.** The finding
held that an `f32` field widened at `init` "bakes the same flags in both precisions from
the same descriptor". It does not, and the reason is not the threshold. Measured on a 5°
fold, the same descriptor, `-Dphysics_f64` off then on: the computed fold cosine is
`0.9961947` at `f32` against `0.9961946981138378` at `f64`, and the flags come out
`0b110` at `f32` and `0b111` at `f64` WITH the fixed `f32` threshold. The compared
quantity is `normalize((v₁−v₀) × (v₂−v₀)) · n_neighbour`, whose own build-dependence is
`2.3e-8` — the same order as the `2.2e-8` between the two renderings of the constant — and
no typing of the threshold touches it. Two further measurements bound the claim: NO `f32`
value lies strictly inside that `2.2e-8` band, the `f32` ULP near 1 being `5.96e-8`, so
with `f32` inputs the old typing could never flip a verdict on its own; and a search over
200 000 folds around 5° found the divergence driven by the geometric term in every case,
with the old and new forms diverging identically. The requested band test is therefore
NOT WRITABLE at the descriptor grain, and was not written — what is written instead is the
type and bit-pattern pin of the constant, the equality pin against the descriptor default,
and the round-trip proof that the widening is exact. The fix stands entirely on the
finding's FIRST reason, configurability, which was correct.
