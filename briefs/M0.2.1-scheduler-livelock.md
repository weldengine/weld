## Context

Hotfix post-M0.2. The test `tests/ecs/no_alloc_steady_state.zig` hangs in `dispatchPhase` (`src/core/ecs/scheduler.zig:640`) with a probability of about 30 % under the full pre-push hook (build + test + test-release). The test invoked directly on its own passes in ~1 s × 5 attempts without hang. The hang only appears under the concurrent load of the pre-push (build + other tests in parallel). Observed twice during M0.2 (27 min then 30 min before manual kill). Pattern: not a simple deadlock, more likely a livelock in the work-stealing, sample stuck on `dispatchPhase` on the main thread side. This debt is listed explicitly in the "Residual risks" of the M0.2 brief.

M0.2.1 delivers a reproducible instrumented diagnostic then the targeted fix (plan A) or the mitigation by targeted revert of an M0.2 feature if the fix requires a refactor beyond the hotfix scope (plan B). M0.2.1 does not consume a slot of the Phase 0 plan (`engine-phase-0-plan.md` remains M0.0–M0.8) and does not open a new sub-phase.

## Scope

## Notes

### Candidate hypotheses to investigate in E1

The hypotheses below are to be investigated by the E1 static analysis. **None is confirmed at this stage.** The brief lists them to fix the scope of the reasoning, not to prejudge the cause.

- **H1 — Contention `drainAtBoundary(.phase)` ↔ work-stealing**: hypothesis that M0.2 inserted an event bus drain on the phase boundaries (`drainAtBoundary` or an equivalent symbol). If this drain holds a synchronization primitive while the workers continue to steal, and the scheduler then waits for "all workers idle" to advance, a circular wait cycle is possible. **Exact existence and signature of the symbol to confirm in E1 by reading the code.**

- **H5 — Observer re-enqueue post-`helpUntilDone`**: ordering variant of H1. If `dispatchPhase` calls a coordination mechanism of the "help until done" type then drains, and an observer dispatched by the drain indirectly produces a command that re-enqueues a job, that job is no longer drained. The scheduler can then block on "workers all idle" which never arrives. **Existence and exact name of the mechanism to confirm in E1.**

- **H2 — Wake lost in the sleep/wake mechanism (debt D-S1-3 absorbed in M0.1)**: M0.1 replaced the busy-yield with sleep/wake (debt D-S1-3 absorbed). Classic edge race on `std.Io.ResetEvent` or condvar: a worker goes to sleep state after the condition check but before `wait`, the scheduler signals in between, wake lost. The concurrent pre-push exposes wider time windows than the direct test.

- **H3 — Drain that ping-pongs an atomic cache line**: even when the events queue is empty, the drain can test an atomic contended with the writers (workers that emit). Under concurrent load, the ping-pong degrades performance but does not on its own explain a hang of several minutes. Aggravation hypothesis, not a primary cause.

- **H4 — Chase-Lev race (ABA, ARM memory orderings)**: very rare bug exposed under precise input distributions. Would manifest as a crash or a negative task counter rather than as a clean livelock in `dispatchPhase` on the main side. Last-resort hypothesis. If E3 concludes H4, that is a Case 2 blocking signal — out-of-scope hotfix, Claude.ai round-trip for a dedicated milestone.

- **H6 — Wave-lifecycle race in the ECS scheduler** *(added post-E2 by Claude.ai round-trip 2026-05-23, replacing H2/H1bis as top-1)*: late `fetchSub` of a wave G worker that hits the `pending_count` counter after wave G+1 has been published — or variant: publication of wave G+1 without a sufficient release fence after all wave G workers have signaled done. Underflow of `pending_count` → wave G+1 never reaches `pending_count == 0`, the main thread spins indefinitely on `publishWaveAndWait`. **Distinct from H4**: H6 lives in the ECS scheduler layer above the Chase-Lev deque — which is NOT stressed in the reproduction (`steals_succeeded = 0` in the E2 dump), which excludes strict H4. The ECS scheduler being a simpler module, the targeted fix is typically < 100 lines — **no out-of-scope hotfix**, plan A remains the likely path. Dump signature: `pending_count = u64::MAX` (underflow), low `parks`, `queue_count` possibly 0.

### E3 residual hypotheses and their discrimination via stack trace + E2ter dump

*Added post-E3 by Claude.ai round-trip 2026-05-23. E3 localized the faulty site to `src/core/jobs/scheduler.zig:333` (`pending_count.fetchSub`) but did not isolate the triggering path of the over-decrement by static analysis alone. The 3 hypotheses below are the remaining candidates — each discriminable by reading the stack trace + dump at the moment of the E2ter panic.*

- **R1 — Wave-lifecycle race** (refinement of H6): panic strikes while another worker is still active on the previous wave, which would cause a late fetchSub decrementing the new wave. **Discriminant**: dump at panic shows ≥ 1 worker with `last_generation` < `scheduler.generation` (workers active on the old wave). The faulty worker itself may have `last_generation` == or < `scheduler.generation` depending on the exact timing.

- **R2 — `std.Io.Condition.waitUncancelable` or `std.Io.Mutex` primitive bug** Zig 0.16: a spurious wait return (without an active broadcast) that pushes the worker to pushShare then pop+fetchSub a phantom job. **Discriminant**: stack trace traces back to the panic immediately from the wait return (or very shortly after), with `parks_completed` having just incremented, and the faulty worker has `last_generation == scheduler.generation` (was just woken on this wave).

- **R3 — Subtle ARM memory-ordering** (Apple Silicon): a worker sees a stale value of `generation` after its fetchSub, which suggests that the `.acq_rel` ordering is insufficient for this pattern. **Discriminant**: `generation.load(.acquire)` at panic differs from the `generation` the worker thought it was processing (capturable if both are explicitly logged in the panic message).

If the E2ter dump clearly discriminates one of these hypotheses (R1, R2, R3), re-opened E3 can conclude without a Claude.ai round-trip. Otherwise, mandatory Claude.ai round-trip (may signal a fourth unconsidered mechanism).

**Resolution post-E2ter (2026-05-23)**: **R1 confirmed by the 2 E2ter dumps** with a precise mechanism. Discriminating signature: the faulty worker systematically has **+1 chunks_processed vs its bracket-peers**, non-deterministic identity (any worker of the bracket can be preempted in the window). Precise triggering path localized: window between `last_generation = cur_gen_quick` (sites `scheduler.zig:350/370/383`) and `pushShare`'s `const n = sched.chunk_count` (`scheduler.zig:396`). Under preemption in this window, the worker resumes with n of the next wave but last_generation of the previous wave → double pushShare for the new wave → double fetchSub → underflow. R1 is a **precise variant of H6** that the dumps made identifiable.

### Recommendations on the E2 instrumentation

The `logging in-loop` mode is explicitly discouraged: it changes the timing of the livelock and can mask it. The right approach is the **state dump at timeout** (Heisenbug-aware): no log before the timeout arms, then exhaustive capture of the state at the moment the livelock is declared. The test exits with `error.SchedulerLivelock` (or an equivalent to be named in E2), not by manual kill.

`Uncancelable` variants to be used systematically for any internal synchronization primitive added to the instrumentation (cf. `engine-zig-conventions.md §11`).

### No CI modification

The pre-push hook remains as is after M0.2.1. No `--workers=N` extension, no addition of `test_stress` to `zig build test` by default. The 100× loop of the stress signal is a local validation tool, not a routine CI test. Decision to reconsider Phase 0.1+ if stability regressions are observed in other milestones.

- 2026-05-23 20:45 (E3 reopened) — **Root cause identified — R1 confirmed** (wave-lifecycle race, precise refinement of the mechanism):

  **Mechanism**: the race window is between `last_generation = cur_gen_quick;` (3 sites `scheduler.zig:350, 370, 383`) and the `const n = sched.chunk_count;` at the entry of `pushShare` (`scheduler.zig:396`). Under preemption in this window:

  1. T0: Wave G published (chunk_count = n_G, generation = G). Worker observes.
  2. T1: Worker does `last_generation = G` (sets local var).
  3. T_preempt: worker is preempted (OS scheduler).
  4. Wave G drains (active workers). pending_count_G → 0. Dispatcher exits.
  5. Wave G+1 published (chunk_count = n_(G+1), gen = G+1). memcpy of `sched.jobs[0..n_(G+1)]` executed.
  6. T2: Worker resumes. Enters `pushShare`. Reads `sched.chunk_count = n_(G+1)` (the new wave's value).
  7. Worker pushes `sched.jobs[worker_idx]` which now contains G+1's data (memcpy up to date).
  8. Worker pop + execute + fetchSub on `pending_count_(G+1)`. **Worker's 1st fetchSub for G+1.**
  9. Worker enters next-iter. Hot path: pop empty. Steal empty. Spin path.
  10. cur_gen_quick = G+1. last_generation = G. **cur_gen_quick != last_generation** → pushShare AGAIN.
  11. last_generation = G+1. pushShare wave G+1: pushes `sched.jobs[worker_idx]` again (same G+1 data).
  12. Worker pop + execute + fetchSub. **Worker's 2nd fetchSub for G+1.**
