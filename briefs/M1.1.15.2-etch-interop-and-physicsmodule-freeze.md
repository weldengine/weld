## Context

This milestone closes the core of Forge 3D. It delivers the mechanism by which an Etch rule
reaches a Tier 1 module and receives its events — a capability that does not exist at any layer
today — then uses that mechanism to drive `forge_3d` from Etch, and then freezes
`PhysicsModule` permanently. C1.0 and C1.1 both gate here.

The milestone carries nine gates because it absorbed a separate milestone. `M1.C — Interop Etch
↔ Tier 1` existed from 2026-08-29 to 2026-08-30 and was dissolved into this one. **The property
that had to survive the dissolution is the ordering, not the identifier:** the generic language
mechanism ships and closes before any physics surface moves, and it is proven on a toy service
and a toy event, never on physics. A mechanism proven by the only caller that motivated it is
indistinguishable from a mechanism cut to fit that caller.

**The freeze is this milestone's attribute and it does not move a third time.** It has already
moved twice.

## Notes

**The count that governs the freeze, and the trap inside it.** `PhysicsModule` carries **32
`assertFn`**, of which **29** exclude `init` / `deinit` / `step`. **The two numbers are distinct
and confusing them has already cost.** The comptime guard bounds the *surface*, so it is the
**32** that bound it; a guard built on 29 passes an implementation missing any one of the three
lifecycle entries. This is not hypothetical — the current file header says so itself, then cites
a §12 that no longer carries its numbers.

**`Forge3DModule` reconciles exactly.** 28 public entries at `4869ef1`, plus the four this
milestone adds — `createJoint`, `destroyJoint`, `setJointMotor`, `getTriggerOverlaps` — equals
32. No phantom entry on either side. `dedupEntities` is an internal helper, not a surface entry.

**Why `setJointMotor` exists.** `engine-physics-forge.md` §5 carries a normative example,
`open_door`, that mutates a motor from Etch. No entry wrote a motor after creation, while motor
targets are posted **per tick** at step 5 bis. Routing the mutation through the component was
rejected: a substitutable `physics_3d` backend sees only `PhysicsModule`, so under that channel
`open_door` drives no custom solver. It is `anyerror!void` and not `void` because it writes the
exact fields `createJoint` validates; an entry writing validated fields with no validation
channel makes the creation guard bypassable by a call. The three pose setters stay `void`
without contradiction — they write `Vec3` / `Quat` whose domain is checked nowhere at creation,
so they bypass no guard.

**The authority model, and why three previous attempts failed.** They all asked *who wrote this
`Transform`*. The ECS stores values, not authorship: the change tick records *when*, never
*who*. Solver-side provenance does not close it either. Authority by `BodyType` is too coarse —
a dynamic body under scripted control has no way to exist. So: stop detecting, declare.
`RigidBody.authority` is `PhysicsAuthority = .solver` for **every** `body_type`, with no
type-dependent default. `.gameplay` is explicit and visible.

The matrix `syncIn` must implement:

| `body_type` | `authority` | `syncIn` | `syncOut` | integrated in `step` |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `dynamic` | `.solver` | nothing | pose and velocity | yes |
| `dynamic` | `.gameplay` | pose and velocity, on change | **nothing** | **yes, result discarded** |
| `kinematic` | `.solver` | nothing | velocity only | no |
| `kinematic` | `.gameplay` | pose and velocity, on change | **nothing** | no |
| `static` | either | pose only, on change | nothing | no |

A gameplay-authoritative dynamic body **stays integrated and resolved** — it keeps its mass,
participates in manifolds, pushes other bodies — and its result is discarded, `syncIn` reposing
it next tick. It behaves as an infinitely heavy externally driven body, which is the expected
semantics of a scripted move on an object that must stay collidable. Removing it from
integration would amount to changing its `BodyType` at runtime, which the solver does not
support and which would destroy its island.

**`authority` is a public component field, so the wrapper cannot be the guardian of the
invariant.** A rule can write `entity.get_mut(RigidBody).authority = .gameplay` directly.
Therefore **`syncIn` detects the field change and performs the transition itself**;
`physics_set_authority` is a convenience that writes the field and nothing else.

**Intent is in the call path, not in the field.** A mutated `Transform` does not say *which*
operation to perform, and the `setBodyTransform` / `moveKinematic` split is contractual — one
derives no velocity, the other derives both. So: a direct ECS mutation of `Transform` maps to
`setBodyTransform`, exact application, no derived velocity; a mutation of `Velocity` maps to the
velocity setters; and `physics_move_kinematic` is an explicit call with an atomic ECS mirror of
both `Transform` and `Velocity`.

**Change-driven, with two conjoint predicates, and the conjunction is the point.** `changed_tick`
filters the bulk without reading values — that is the question the tick answers correctly. Value
comparison then runs before the setter, because `getMut` marks `changed_tick` unconditionally: a
system taking a `getMut` without changing anything would mark, and the tick alone would wake for
nothing. `syncOut` already implements exactly this discipline in the same file — *read first,
`getMut` only on a real difference* — with its motive written out. `syncIn` mirrors it; it does
not invent it.

**Normative tick order, on which the diagnostic's correctness entirely depends:**

```
gameplay rules and systems   (write Transform, Velocity)
  → syncIn                   (consume changes, mark the journal)
  → PhysicsModule.step
  → syncOut                  (publish per body_type × authority)
```

The diagnostic reads **"ECS mutation forbidden under solver authority"**, never "a rule wrote".
The tick establishes that a component mutated since the last `syncIn`; a Zig system or the future
`TransformSystem` may have done it as readily as a rule.

**`RigidBody` gains a field, and the `.sav` schema changes with it.** The format embeds a Schema
Registry of field descriptors and the loader detects a divergent schema to apply a migration.
The field has a default, so the migration is a defaulted one — but it must be **written**, not
assumed.

**The parser-mode arbitration, already settled — implement it, do not re-litigate it.**
`E1900` is a parser-mode check; `E1901` is post-parse. `E1900` bears on the *form* of a `fn`.
`E1901` bears on the *identity* of an already-parsed top-level construct, which is what
`src/etch/scene_cook.zig` already handles with its own error set; giving it a parser path would
duplicate a twenty-construct list that is enumerable from the AST. **Write the settled split into
`etch-grammar.md` §20.3**, whose current wording says only "a parser mode", or the next reading
finds a divergence there.

**A reserve already lifted, measured — do not re-verify it.** The tree-walker does implement
`try` / `catch`: `try_catch_stmt` in `execStmt` of `src/etch/interp.zig` with unwind frames, and
`thrown` / `thrown_value` in flight, exercised by `tests/etch_interp/programs/48_error_throw_catch.etch`
and `58_closure_thrown_propagation.etch`. Declared residual: nothing yet proves it catches a
**Zig error union converted to a `throw`**, since no service exists. That is G2's exit.

**Debt lives in the plan, in one place, and code points to it.** Do not write prose paragraphs
into code comments. Two of the last three findings of the previous milestone were about prose
that had no business being in the code.

**Discipline carried from M1.1.15.1 — eleven instrument defects, seven review rounds, and not one
of the eleven was found by a red check that named it.** All were found by re-reading a number
already reported green.

- **A conclusion is worth what its perimeter enumeration is worth.** Four times a green count
  came out of a predicate weaker than the claim it supported. State the predicate **before**
  counting, and confront it word for word with what it must support.
- **A claim is re-derived from the spec, never from the last counter-example.** Five successive
  formulations, each narrowed to cover the previous counter-example, each meeting the next.
- **A property a layer cannot observe, it cannot guarantee.** It is abandoned, and its proof goes
  to the component that sees it.
- **A guard must be shown able to fail on the property it claims**, not merely able to fail.
- **A counterfactual must be shown to have run**, not merely to have been applied.
- **A sweep classifies by line and substitutes by occurrence** — and grep under-reaches across
  directories while over-reaching across names. Only reading separates.
- **Finding admissibility has a named owner:** whoever receives a review verdict decides its
  admissibility **before** relaying it. Seven review rounds last milestone, four of them with no
  behaviour change at all, with a threshold written in the brief from day one and invoked by
  nobody until the external reviewer applied it himself.

### G4 — Tier 0 event bridge and the tick ordering

- **2026-08-31 01:20** — **The ORDER is the deliverable, so the drain lives inside `stepOnce`.**
  Registered sources are drained AFTER the per-tick `clear` and BEFORE rule dispatch, at the
  position `fireTimers` already occupies. A caller draining before `runFor` would push on the

  observed, and no red anywhere. Putting the drain in the engine makes the ordering a property of
  the engine instead of a discipline the caller has to remember. Placed AHEAD of the timers

- **2026-08-31 01:20** — **THE COUNTERFACTUAL RAN.** The drain was moved to the other side of the
